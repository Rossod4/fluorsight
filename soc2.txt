#### What is PFAS?

Per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances (PFAS), known as "forever chemicals," are a group of synthetic chemicals which, due to the strength of the carbon-fluorine bond are persistent, pose critical environmental and public health challenges through contamination of water, soil, and ecosystems globally.

They have been in use since the 1940s in a wide range of industries from Aqueous Film Forming Foams (AFFFs) and the chemicals industry, through to photography, electronics, food packaging and textiles and many more, and there are now, depending on the definition used, many thousands of these chemicals in existence. Due in part to them having both hydrophobic and hydrophilic properties, as well as being persistent, they are also in many cases highly mobile with increasing evidence that many are toxic and bioaccumulative.

#### UK Regulations & Compliance Testing

Current UK guidance establishes a recommended limit of 0.1 µg/L for the sum of 48 PFAS in drinking water, though no legally binding limits exist yet in England and Wales. The Drinking Water Inspectorate requires water to remain "wholesome" and free from substances posing potential human health risks, creating compliance obligations for water companies and industrial operators.

UK REACH restrictions already cover specific substances like PFOA and PFOS, while broader PFAS restrictions are under development for firefighting foam applications. Environmental permits increasingly incorporate PFAS monitoring requirements, particularly affecting sites with historical use of aqueous film forming foams.
