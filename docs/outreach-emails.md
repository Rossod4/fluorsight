# Fluorsight Outreach Emails

Send-ready templates for the University of Bristol Fluorsight team (chemistry,
aerospace engineering, maths — Faculty of Science and Engineering), for the
**Aegis Innovation Competition 2026**. Goal: replace the assumed **£250/sample**
UK LC-MS/MS PFAS cost with a real figure, and get at least one real user
conversation, before the **4 September 2026** deadline.

Every email is a genuine student research enquiry — not a sales pitch, not a
company. Copy-paste, fill in the `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`, personalise the
recipient's name and one specific detail about their organisation, and send.

---

## Covering note: realistic expectations

- **Reply rates.** Cold outreach from a student project to labs/consultancies
  typically lands **10–20%** response rates, lower for busy senior staff at
  large consultancies, higher for lab sales/technical teams (they're used to
  quote requests). To get **2–4 useful replies**, send to roughly **12–20**
  targets spread across all three categories — don't rely on one email to one
  contact.
- **Send now, not later.** Send the first batch **this week (by 8 August)**.
  That leaves a week for a first reply, a 7-day follow-up (~15 August), and
  still 2+ weeks of buffer before 4 September to write up whatever you get.
- **Order of sending:** Send **B (consultancies)** and **C (local authority)**
  first — a 20-minute call needs more lead time to schedule than a price
  quote. Send **A (labs)** a day or two after; lab sales/technical teams
  usually reply fastest (quote requests are routine for them).
- **Personalise every email.** Swap in the recipient's actual name, and
  reference one real thing about their company (e.g. "I saw your Environmental
  Testing team offers PFAS analysis under UKAS accreditation") — form-letter
  templates get ignored more than tailored ones.
- **A rough number beats no reply.** Every email below has an explicit "even a
  rough figure / one-line answer helps" fallback — use it, it converts silence
  into a quick reply far more often than a request for a precise, complete answer.

## A. To UK analytical labs

**Targets:** ALS Global (Environmental), SOCOTEC UK, Eurofins UK
(Environmental Testing), i2 Analytical. Also worth adding: RPS, Fera Science,
and any other UKAS-accredited UK lab offering PFAS water analysis — search
"UKAS accredited PFAS water testing UK" or the UKAS lab search tool.

**Who to send to:** Technical Manager, Laboratory Manager, or Environmental
Testing Sales/Customer Services. General "request a quote" or enquiries@
addresses on the lab's website work fine for this — quote requests get routed
correctly. If you want a named contact, search LinkedIn for "[Company]
Technical Manager Environmental" or "[Company] Laboratory Manager PFAS".

**Subject line:** `Quick PFAS water-testing pricing question — Bristol student project`

**Body:**

> Dear [Lab Contact Name],
>
> We're three undergraduates at the University of Bristol (chemistry,
> aerospace engineering, and maths) building a decision-support tool for PFAS
> water screening, for a university innovation competition — an academic
> project, not a commercial approach.
>
> Our cost model currently rests on an assumed £250 per sample for UK PFAS
> confirmatory analysis, and we'd like to replace that guess with a real
> figure before our submission on [DEADLINE DATE].
>
> Could you share, even roughly:
> - Typical per-sample price for PFAS analysis in drinking/ground/surface
>   water (and which method/analyte suite, e.g. LC-MS/MS, EA PFAS list)
> - Standard turnaround time
> - Any volume discount structure for batches
>
> We're happy to anonymise the figure in our materials if you'd prefer not to
> be named — this is purely for academic use. Even a single rough number
> would be a huge help.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [Student Name]
> University of Bristol, Faculty of Science and Engineering
> [University email address]

**7-day follow-up (one line):**

> Hi [Name], just following up on my note below re: PFAS testing pricing —
> even a rough per-sample figure or ballpark range would really help ahead of
> our [DATE] competition deadline. Thank you!

## B. To UK environmental consultancies

**Targets:** RSK, WSP, Arcadis, Ramboll, Wood, and similar UK geoenvironmental
consultancies.

**Who to send to:** An Associate Director, Principal Consultant, or Senior
Consultant in the Contaminated Land / Geoenvironmental team — not general
sales or graduate recruitment. Find them via the consultancy's website
"our experts" / team pages for that division, or LinkedIn search "[Company]
contaminated land principal consultant" or "[Company] geoenvironmental
associate director".

**Subject line:** `20 minutes on how you triage PFAS samples? (Bristol student project)`

## C. To a local authority contaminated land officer

**Who to send to:** The Contaminated Land Officer or Environmental Health
Officer (Land Contamination/Pollution Control) at a local authority. Find via
the council's "Environmental Health" or "Contaminated Land" webpage — most
publish a direct email or team inbox there; otherwise use the general
environmental.health@[council].gov.uk address and ask to be redirected.

**Subject line:** `PFAS & Part 2A workload — quick question from Bristol students`

**Body:**

> Dear [Name],
>
> We're three University of Bristol undergraduates building a PFAS
> water-screening triage tool for a university innovation competition — an
> academic project, not a sales approach.
>
> We're trying to understand how councils are currently handling PFAS under
> Part 2A contaminated land duties, given Defra's 2026 PFAS Plan and the
> upcoming EA prioritisation map. Before [DEADLINE DATE], could you answer a
> few short questions — by email is fine, no call needed:
>
> 1. Has PFAS come onto your team's radar under Part 2A yet, or is it still
>    emerging?
> 2. Roughly how many potentially contaminated sites is your authority
>    tracking?
> 3. What's the biggest constraint — budget, lab capacity, staff time, or
>    something else?
> 4. How would your team currently decide which sites justify paying for
>    lab-confirmed PFAS testing?
> 5. Would a free/low-cost tool that pre-screens site risk before lab
>    confirmation be useful?
>
> Even one-line answers would help enormously — this is the single biggest
> gap in our evidence base right now.
>
> Thank you for your time,
> [Student Name]
> University of Bristol, Faculty of Science and Engineering
> [University email address]

**7-day follow-up (one line):**

> Hi [Name], just checking in on the note below — even brief, informal
> answers would really help our university project. Thank you for your time!
