## 1. Eurofins Chemtest — sales@etuki.eurofins.com

**Subject:** Quick PFAS water-testing pricing question — Bristol student project

Dear Chemtest team,

We're three undergraduates at the University of Bristol (chemistry, aerospace engineering, and maths) building a decision-support tool for PFAS water screening, for a university innovation competition — an academic project, not a commercial approach. Chemtest came up in our research into UK laboratories offering PFAS analysis in water, so we hoped you might be able to help.

Our cost model currently rests on an assumed £250 per sample for UK PFAS confirmatory analysis, and we'd like to replace that guess with a real figure before our submission on 11 September.

Could you share, even roughly:

- Typical per-sample price for PFAS analysis in drinking/ground/surface water (and which method/analyte suite, e.g. LC-MS/MS, EA PFAS list)
- Standard turnaround time
- Any volume discount structure for batches

We're happy to anonymise the figure in our materials if you'd prefer not to be named — this is purely for academic use. Even a single rough number would be a huge help.

Thank you for your time,

## 5. RSK Geosciences — geosciences@rsk.co.uk

**Subject:** 20 minutes on how you triage PFAS samples? (Bristol student project)

Dear RSK Geosciences team,

We're three University of Bristol undergraduates (chemistry, aerospace engineering, maths) building "Fluorsight" — an explainable triage tool that helps decide which water samples justify expensive LC-MS/MS PFAS confirmation — for a university innovation competition, not a commercial pitch. RSK Geosciences came up in our research into UK contaminated-land consultancies, and we noticed you have an office here in Bristol.

We haven't yet spoken to anyone who does this work day-to-day, and that's the biggest gap in our submission. Could we have 20 minutes of someone's time before 11 September, by call or even email, to ask:

1. How do you currently decide which samples get sent for confirmatory PFAS analysis?
2. Roughly what does that cost per project or site?
3. Do budget constraints ever force you to under-sample or under-test?
4. Would a transparent, explainable pre-screening score (before lab confirmation) be useful in your workflow?
5. What would make such a tool unusable or untrustworthy for your work?

If a call doesn't work, even a few written lines in reply would be genuinely useful to us.

Thank you,
Alex Gard
University of Bristol, Faculty of Science and Engineering

## 9. Bristol City Council — pollution@bristol.gov.uk

**Subject:** PFAS & Part 2A workload — quick question from Bristol students

Dear Public Protection (Pollution) team,

We're three University of Bristol undergraduates building a PFAS water-screening triage tool for a university innovation competition — an academic project, not a sales approach. As Bristol students, we wanted to start by asking our own city's team.

We're trying to understand how councils are currently handling PFAS under Part 2A contaminated land duties, given Defra's 2026 PFAS Plan and the upcoming EA prioritisation map. Before 11 September, could you answer a few short questions — by email is fine, no call needed:

1. Has PFAS come onto your team's radar under Part 2A yet, or is it still emerging?
2. Roughly how many potentially contaminated sites is your authority tracking?
3. What's the biggest constraint — budget, lab capacity, staff time, or something else?
4. How would your team currently decide which sites justify paying for lab-confirmed PFAS testing?
5. Would a free/low-cost tool that pre-screens site risk before lab confirmation be useful?

Even one-line answers would help enormously — this is the single biggest gap in our evidence base right now.

Thank you for your time,
Alex Gard
University of Bristol, Faculty of Science and Engineering
