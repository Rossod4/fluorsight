# If you do nothing else, do these two

You said primary research probably isn't happening, and asked me to flag anything genuinely worth
the effort. I'm not going to re-pitch the whole list. Two items survive an honest cost/benefit
test, and I'd argue for them specifically because **neither is really "research"** — they're an
afternoon of messaging between them.

The competition marks *adoption / delivered impact*, *interviews* and *market surveys* as named
sub-criteria. All three currently score **zero**, because nothing outside the team has ever touched
this project. That is roughly **3 marks of the poster's 30** sitting unclaimed, plus the knock-on
to the oral's feasibility criterion.

---

## 1. Get five people to open the live app · ~1 hour total · **do this one**

**The ask:** message five people — coursemates, a lecturer, a family friend who works in
engineering, anyone. *"Can you spend three minutes on this and tell me the first thing that
confused you?"* Link: `https://rossod4.github.io/AEGIS/#/demo`

**Log for each:** date, first name, role/discipline, and one verbatim sentence of what they said.

**Why this is the single best-value thing on the list:**
- It converts *adoption / delivered impact* from a zero into a scoring line. The poster can then
  say "eight people outside the team have run it; here is what two of them said" instead of
  claiming impact it cannot evidence.
- It is the closest available echo of what actually won in 2025 — judges praised DrugWise for
  "the availability of the prototype", and availability means *other people used it*, not that it
  was deployed.
- A verbatim quote from a real user is worth more on a poster than any statistic you can model,
  and you cannot manufacture one later.
- If someone finds the app confusing, **you want to know that before a judge does.** This is a
  usability test as much as an evidence exercise.

**Cost:** five WhatsApp messages and the patience to write down what came back.

## 2. Send the lab emails · ~20 minutes · **worth it, and already written**

The emails are drafted and ready in `outreach-emails.md`. Sending five costs about twenty minutes
of copy-paste and personalisation.

**Why it's worth it even if nobody replies:** the non-reply *is* the finding. "No UK laboratory
publishes a PFAS rate card — we contacted five and none would quote without a project reference"
is a legitimate market-survey result, it is honest, and it directly supports the decision to model
at £250 against the only published UK figure of £350. You cannot make that statement unless you
actually asked.

If one lab does reply with a number, it replaces the largest modelled assumption in your entire
business case with a real one.

## What I'd skip, and why

**The three practitioner interviews.** Genuinely valuable, but they need scheduling, chasing, and
a call you have to turn up to — real calendar cost during term-time run-up. The mentor call on
5 August substitutes partially: your mentor is a sector-adjacent professional, so their answers on
customer and pricing can be cited as expert input, provided you attribute it honestly as one
mentor conversation rather than dressing it up as market research.

**Anything requiring lab access.** Not happening by September, and the entry is honest about
being design-stage. Don't chase it.
