# Full project audit — 4–7 August 2026

Scope: the live website, the app code, the A0 poster, the 7-slide deck and its script, and all ten
supporting documents. Findings are priced on two axes — **marks at stake** (in bands, because
fractional marks are fake precision on an 8-point human-judged scale) and a **credibility flag**,
because this is winner-takes-all and single-shot, so a fabrication costs the entry rather than a
mark.

Every entry states its **evidence-of-done**, because the dominant defect class on this project is a
correction recorded as complete that left survivors elsewhere.

---

## 1. Why this audit was run the way it was

A previous sweep of two files (`Landing.tsx`, `Why.tsx`) checked 149 claims and found **66
problems — a 44% defect rate**, including a fabricated author list carrying a "verified against
primary source" tag. That rate is a property of unsigned AI-generated copy, not of those two files,
and it had never been measured anywhere else. Alex confirmed the original site was generated and
never signed off. So the audit's default posture was: **anything Alex did not personally say is
untrusted until a primary source is retrieved and quoted.**

Three rules followed, each written against a specific past failure:

- **Plausibility is not verification.** It passed a fabricated author list and a category error.
- **A fix needs its own source.** A finding may propose "change X to Y" only if Y carries its own
  fetch-and-quote. Otherwise it may say "delete" or "soften" — nothing else.
- **Recency beats frequency.** Three documents repeating a retracted figure are not evidence; they
  are one unpropagated fix.

That third rule mattered immediately: the git ledger showed "1.8m analyses" had been **deleted**
from the live site as wrong, so the website's 1.7m was the correction and the research docs were
the stale copy — the opposite of what a majority vote would have concluded.

## 3. The finding the audit itself got wrong — and how it was caught

The artefact claim register flagged, as **"the single highest-severity item,"** that the poster
claims a June probe switch while `chemistry-brief-for-dom.md` and `qa-bank.md` both state *"That
never happened — the probe was always 2,6-ANS."* It recommended changing the poster.

**Following that would have deleted the team's best design-evolution story.** Alex confirmed
directly: the team switched from 1,8-ANS to 2,6-ANS in June, on binding grounds, and the 17 July
paper independently confirmed it afterwards. The two documents carried an earlier misreading of
Dom's message.

Corrected in `chemistry-brief-for-dom.md`, `qa-bank.md` and `design-evolution-log.md`, each with an
explicit note not to reintroduce either wrong version. The sequence — **decision in June,
literature in July** — is now also spoken on slide 2, which the rubric scorer identified as "the
highest-value sentence not currently being spoken."

**This was one of four times verification protected a correct claim from a confident wrong fix.**
The others: "8,1-ANS" is the paper's own naming, not a typo (I was about to "correct" it across
eight files); "eight site factors" is right; and the poster's "raising the threshold from 5 to 72
cuts referrals from 21 samples to one" is exactly right, confirmed by running the engine.

## 7. What the deterministic passes found

**Fix-propagation ledger** — mined 17 correction commits, extracted the values each diff deleted,
grepped the repo for survivors. Six of the eight problems that scoped this audit were the same bug:
a fix applied in some places and recorded as done. This pass is cheap, mechanical, and should be
re-run before submission.

**Mutation testing** — perturbed the implementation and re-ran the suite. Three surviving mutants:
`thresholds.medium` 30 → 15, the Critical band comparison `>=` → `>`, and `GUIDELINE_NG_L` 100 → 50
all pass all 27 tests. The suite tests scoring arithmetic well and decision boundaries not at all.

**Engine re-derivation** — ran the real engine against the seed data. Confirmed: 23 samples, 21
screened, 3 lab-confirmed, **5 escalated at threshold 50 = 23.8%**, and 21 escalated at threshold 5
falling to 1 at threshold 72. The economics chain re-derives exactly: 500 × £250 = £125,000
all-lab; 100 analyses + £5,000 subscription + £20,000 consumables = £50,000; saving £75,000 =
**60.0%**. Also confirmed 1,840 ng/L = 18.4× the guideline.

**One defect the probe found:** the deck's spoken driver readout said "Screening evidence,
thirty-one points of forty-five." The real value for RM-FTG-001 is **35 of 35**. The other two
numbers Alex speaks — airfield 10 of 10, sensitivity 5 of 10 — are exactly right.
