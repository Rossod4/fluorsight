1Scope and impact

Nobody can afford to analyse everything, so on most sites most samples never are. Which ones do get analysed is decided when the budget runs out — on desk study and professional judgement, and it is almost never written down.

Goal. No PFAS investigation in Britain limited by not knowing which samples mattered. This year. A working decision layer, publicly reachable today, that ranks samples and records why.

3What a screen cannot do

Fluorescence cannot be a compliance instrument. A single probe measures total hydrophobic-anion burden — detergents and humic acids interfere — so it cannot identify which PFAS is present, and we do not claim it can.

That is the argument for triage, not against it. The output is a risk score, never a concentration, and every escalation goes to accredited LC-MS/MS. Cross-reactivity is a disclosed property of a screening instrument, not a hidden defect.

4What the threshold is worth

On a 500-sample annual programme, triage cuts modelled spend from £125,000 to £50,000 — a £75,000 saving. Aegis is £5,000/year plus £40/sample consumables.

Escalation rate is our weakest assumption, so we publish the range rather than a point: 10% → £87,500 · 20% → £75,000 · 40% → £50,000. The case survives at double our assumption.

Lab cost modelled at £250/sample — an assumption, not a quote. No UK laboratory publishes a PFAS rate card; we approached seven and all quote on request.

2How the decision gets made

Measure. A β-cyclodextrin cartridge preconcentrates roughly 250-fold, then a 2,6-ANS probe reports displacement as a fluorescence shift. Output: a signal and a confidence flag.

Score. Ten weighted factors — screening evidence, repeat positives, airfield and landfill history, receptor sensitivity. Every point is attributed to a named driver.

Choose. The threshold, not the chemistry, sets the budget. Defend. Each sample carries its score, its drivers, and the threshold in force.

5Development and design evolution

Weekly meetings since 1 June, with a written decision record. Three disciplines: chemistry and method (Dom), product and field use (Evan), risk model and cost case (Alex). Registered as five; two never engaged, so three absorbed the scope.

17 Jul. A new binding study confirmed 2,6-ANS pairs best with β-cyclodextrin — the pairing we had chosen. Aug. We found our engine scored signal and concentration band separately when they are one reading; merging them means no single measurement can escalate a sample alone.

The one dial — and where it breaks

Inset right: escalation queue, 21 ranked samples with a heavy rule at the threshold, ESCALATE above / HOLD below.

Every triage tool has one dial: turn it up and you save money, turn it too far and you miss contamination. Most products hide it. Ours is on screen and movable. On our demo portfolio, escalation falls from 100% of screened samples to 9.5% with nothing missed.

Shows the shape of the trade-off — a structural property of the scoring model — not our accuracy. Demo data is synthetic and written by us. Back-test n = 3: one true positive (lab returned 1,840 ng/L, 18× the guideline), two true negatives, no misses. We report the method and the n, not a rate.

A triage layer, not a laboratory replacement.

6Where this could fail

Strengths

  • Working prototype, publicly deployed
  • Every score explained driver by driver
  • Method-agnostic — any chemistry fits behind it

Weaknesses

  • No bench data → first pilot must produce it
  • Weights asserted, not calibrated → paired study
  • One probe cannot speciate → array roadmap

Opportunities

  • EA map reaches councils end-2026 → our pipeline
  • England statutory limit consulted on in 2026
  • Consumable margin as a second revenue line

Threats

  • Cyclopure sells a full mail-in test at $85
  • Labs could bundle a cheap screen themselves
  • UKAS treatment of non-accredited screens unconfirmed

7Next steps

Sensitivity. Methylated β-cyclodextrin raises ANS fluorescence roughly 120-fold against 8.4-fold for the unmodified host, and gives the tightest binding reported for our probe. With temperature control, that is our route toward the guideline.

Selectivity. Four probes instead of one, classified by linear discriminant analysis — the published route to telling PFAS apart. We already have the software half that most sensor projects lack.

Validation. Paired screening and LC-MS/MS on archived samples from a partner consultancy. Kills it if no partner by December — we publish at n = 3 and say so.

Fallback market. If we cannot reach 0.1 µg/L, AFFF source zones at 10 µg/L–mg/L are reachable today, and are where our heaviest site weighting already points.

Aegis — PFAS screening triage. Aegis is decision support. Screening estimates carry uncertainty and are never a substitute for accredited LC-MS/MS or used for regulatory compliance reporting. All demo data is synthetic.
